§1.  Overview and reading order
Block 1 established the surrogate-level claim: v3 is a control-oriented black-box surrogate with poor multi-horizon predictive fidelity (24h RMSET = 1.557 °C), while calibrated v3.5 is a physically-identified surrogate with strong predictive fidelity (24h RMSET = 0.644 °C). Block 2 then tests the controller-side question: which combinations of these two surrogates produce useful Reinforcement-Learning policies on the live BOPTEST RTE bestest_air testcase, and which fail.
The Block 2 narrative across this document follows the controller-family stack:
§2 Pure v3 PPO baseline — the control-oriented surrogate alone produces a working policy.
§3 Direct v3.5 PPO (negative control) — using the high-fidelity surrogate alone fails catastrophically.
§4 Thermostatic hybrid — combining v3 dynamics with a v3.5 disagreement regularizer is the best result.
§5 HDRL sweep — the same hybrid regularization does NOT transfer to hierarchical RL; lambda_temp_disagree must be 0.
§6 MORL 5D failure / 17D success — observation interface choice dominates the MORL result.
§7 MORL canonical seed analysis — N=5 seed variance falsifies the single-seed canonical result.
§8 MORL Pareto sweep — comfort/energy trade-off across 5 weight pairs.
§9 PI baseline — BOPTEST built-in reference.
§10 Transfer diagnostics — closed-loop surrogate-vs-live ms_gap and feature attribution.
§11 Synthesis — fidelity-vs-utility paradox and Block 3 prerequisites.
Common to all results in Block 2: comfort band 21 °C – 24 °C, step = 900 s, rollout duration = 14 days, live testcase = BOPTEST bestest_air (Docker BOPTEST RTE HTTP).
§2.  Pure v3 PPO thermostatic baseline
§2.1  Setup
The pure v3 PPO baseline uses the canonical v3 control-oriented surrogate (outputs/surrogatev2/rcnodev3tsupply.pt) as the rollout dynamics inside PPO, with no v3.5 disagreement regularization (lambdatempdisagree = 0, lambdapowerdisagree = 0). The policy is a thermostatic PPO actor with a comfort-centered reward; observations include the 17-dimensional TSup-style feature vector (zone temperature, ambient, solar, ahead-of-day occupancy, hour/day cyclic, lagged power, etc.). After training on the surrogate, the policy is deployed against the live BOPTEST RTE for 14-day rollouts on two targeted scenarios.
§2.2  Live BOPTEST results
Pure v3 PPO reaches sub-degree comfort RMSE in both targeted-window scenarios (peak: 0.894 °C; typical: 0.745 °C) and an ms well below the thermostat or PI baselines (peak ms = 0.156, typical = 0.126). This is the first central finding of Block 2: a surrogate that is provably inaccurate as a 24-hour predictor (Block 1 §1.3) nonetheless produces a usable PPO policy with single-digit setpoint violation percentage on the live test cases.
§3.  Direct v3.5 PPO — negative control
§3.1  Hypothesis and configuration
The naive scientific hypothesis would be: since v3.5 is the higher-fidelity surrogate (Block 1: 0.644 °C 24h RMSE vs v3's 1.557 °C), a PPO trained on v3.5 should produce a strictly better controller. We test this by training PPO directly on the calibrated v3.5 backbone with no v3 dynamics (lambdatempdisagree = 0, no hybrid mixing), then deploying on the live BOPTEST RTE.
§3.2  Result: catastrophic failure
Direct v3.5 PPO produces m_s = 1.090 on peak and 1.120 on typical scenarios — an order of magnitude worse than pure v3 PPO. Setpoint violation reaches 81.2 % on peak, with comfort RMSE of 4.36 °C — the policy spends most of the rollout outside the 21–24 °C band.
This is the fidelity-vs-utility paradox in its clearest form: the surrogate with better predictive accuracy (v3.5, RMSET = 0.644 °C) produces a substantially worse PPO policy than the surrogate with worse predictive accuracy (v3, RMSET = 1.557 °C). Mechanistically, v3.5's sharp physical predictions yield high-variance gradient estimates that destabilise PPO advantage learning, while v3's smoother, bias-toward-mean predictions are gentler on the gradient signal.
§3.3  Warm-start utility check
We additionally check whether v3.5 has any value as a warm-start initialiser (pre-train on v3.5, then fine-tune on the hybrid backend). The comparison table below shows that scratch training on the hybrid backend outperforms warm-starting from v3.5 by a wide margin on both scenarios.
Table 2 — Direct-v3.5 warm-start utility comparison.  From outputs/block2_thermostatic_warmstart_utility/comparison_summary.csv.
Warm-starting from v3.5 not only fails to help — it actively harms training, raising m_s by 2–3×. Conclusion: v3.5 must be used as a soft regularizer (next section), not as a direct rollout backbone or warm-start source.
§4.  Thermostatic hybrid (canonical positive result)
§4.1  Architecture: v3 dynamics + v3.5 disagreement regularizer
The hybrid backend uses v3 as primary rollout dynamics (so PPO sees the smooth, control-friendly transitions) and v3.5 as a soft disagreement regularizer: at each policy step the v3.5 prediction is evaluated alongside v3's, and the policy loss is augmented with a temperature-disagreement term (lambdatempdisagree = 0.1) and a power-disagreement term (lambdapowerdisagree = 0.00005). The disagreement is a censor, not a forecast: when v3 and v3.5 disagree strongly, the policy is penalised; when they agree, the loss reduces to vanilla PPO.
§4.2  Live BOPTEST results
Hybridl010 achieves ms = 0.033 (peak) and 0.021 (typical) — a 4–6× reduction relative to pure v3 — with comfort RMSE down to 0.633 / 0.612 °C and setpoint violation under 1.5 % on both scenarios. Energy consumption is essentially unchanged from pure v3 (311 vs 330 kWh peak); the hybrid achieves better comfort at no energy cost.
Table 4 — Canonical Block 2 results on the live BOPTEST RTE (paper §6.3).  Sources: outputs/block13_closed_loop_transfer_{pure_v3, no_delta_t_powerlog, hybrid_l010}/summary.csv. Green = strong result (m_s < 0.10); red = failure (m_s > 0.50); yellow = intermediate.
The Block 2 main table shows the full fidelity-vs-utility paradox in a single panel: hybrid (v3+v3.5 reg) > pure v3 > direct v3.5. Predictive fidelity does not determine controller quality — the role of the calibrated physical twin (v3.5) is to provide a soft physics censor, not to be the rollout dynamics.
§4.3  Hybrid disagreement bounded, not chaotic
On the canonical hybrid traces, the average temperature disagreement between v3 and v3.5 is 0.969 °C (p95 = 2.52 °C); the average power disagreement is 708 W (p95 = 1236 W). These bounds confirm that the two surrogates are not chaotically divergent — they remain in the same dynamical regime, so the disagreement signal is informative rather than dominated by representation noise.
§5.  HDRL sweep — controller-family-specific limit
§5.1  Question
Does the thermostatic-optimal lambdatempdisagree = 0.10 transfer to the next controller family — Hierarchical Deep RL (HDRL)? HDRL has a different action space (multi-level scheduler + low-level setpoint controller) and different gradient dynamics, so a universal hybrid weight is not guaranteed.
§5.2  Sweep results
Table 5 — HDRL sweep across λ_temp_disagree ∈ {0, 0.03, 0.05, 0.10}.  Green = best (λ = 0.00); red = worst (λ = 0.10). From reports/block2_hdrl_lambda_sweep_summary.csv.
On both scenarios, HDRL performance monotonically degrades with increasing lambdatempdisagree. The best HDRL setting is lambda = 0 (no temperature regularization). The thermostatic-default lambda = 0.10 produces a 2.4× worse ms on peak and a 2.2× worse ms on typical compared to lambda = 0.
§5.3  Interpretation: hybrid regularization is family-specific
The HDRL sweep falsifies the hypothesis that there is a single optimal lambdatempdisagree across controller families. The right reading is: thermostatic PPO benefits from temperature disagreement censoring, but HDRL — which already has a hierarchical comfort-aware structure — is over-regularised by the same signal. The lesson for the MORL stage (§6) is that the power-only hybrid backend (lambdatemp = 0, lambdapower = 5e-5) should be the starting point, not the temperature-and-power hybrid that worked for thermostatic PPO.
§6.  MORL 5D failure → 17D success
§6.1  The frozen 5D failure (Section 6.5 of roadmap)
The first MORL implementation used a 5-dimensional observation (zone temperature, ambient temperature, hour, day, occupancy). Both training and live evaluation produced comfort RMSE of 4.96 °C with 74.5 % setpoint violations — a complete failure. The policy could not use the available observations to distinguish heating from cooling demand or to anticipate occupancy transitions. This result is preserved as a frozen artifact (audit anchor only) and is not re-generated by the current code path.
§6.2  The 17D power-only backend (canonical MORL)
Replacing the 5D observation with the 17-dimensional TSup-style feature vector — same as thermostatic PPO and HDRL — and disabling temperature disagreement regularization (lambdatemp = 0, lambdapower = 5e-5, learned from the HDRL sweep in §5) recovers a usable MORL policy: ms = 0.099, 4.9 % violation, RMSET = 0.72 °C, energy = 248.6 kWh.
Table 6 — MORL 5D vs 17D comparison.  Red = failure; green = canonical success. From reports/block2_morl_comparison_summary.csv.
The MORL result confirms the cross-family pattern: v3 supplies the temperature dynamics, v3.5 supplies a soft power censor, and the controller-family-specific lambda values come from the HDRL sweep. The dominant factor in MORL success was not the backend (it was the same hybrid in both 5D and 17D), but the observation-interface design.
§7.  MORL canonical seed analysis (N = 5 falsification)
§7.1  Pre-registration and audit anchors
The MORL canonical seed at the time of audit anchor 93df9b3 (commit 93df9b3) was seed 42 with comfort/energy weights 50/50, reaching m_s = 0.099 on the single-seed yearly evaluation. The single-seed result is reproducible but does not characterise the underlying random-seed distribution. Audit anchor 62dc859 (commit 62dc859) extends this to N = 5 seeds and reports the seed-variance result below.
Table 7 — MORL N=5 seed variance on yearly BOPTEST evaluation.  Red CV = high relative variance (≥ 40 %). From reports/morl_canonical_seedfix_yearly_summary.csv.
§7.2  Finding: high seed variance, no single-seed superiority
Across N = 5 seeds at the 50/50 weight pair, ms ranges from 0.103 to 0.310 (mean 0.187, CV = 0.42). The 75/25 weight pair has even higher CV (0.61), driven by occasional outlier seeds that fail to converge to the comfort region. The single-seed canonical (ms = 0.099, seed 42) is the best of five — not representative of the median MORL policy.
This falsifies the strong reading of MORL canonical results ("MORL produces ms ≈ 0.10 reliably") and supports the weaker but defensible reading ("MORL produces ms in [0.10, 0.31] depending on seed; we report the best single seed as the pre-registered canonical"). The N = 5 finding is logged in audit anchor 62dc859 and is referenced in §6.5 of the paper.
§8.  MORL Pareto sweep
§8.1  Weight pairs
The Pareto sweep evaluates five (comfort, energy) weight pairs on the same MORL 17D power-only backend: 0/100, 25/75, 50/50, 75/25, 100/0. For each pair, training is run to the same number of policy updates and evaluated on the yearly BOPTEST scenario set.
Table 8 — MORL Pareto front (seed 42, yearly evaluation).  Green = strong (m_s < 0.15); red = failure (m_s > 0.5). From reports/morl_pareto_front_table.csv.
§8.2  Interpretation
The Pareto front shows: (a) the 0/100 (energy-only) policy collapses to a degenerate near-zero-power solution with 87 % violation — energy is minimised by simply not heating; (b) the 100/0 (comfort-only) policy is the best ms but spends the most energy; (c) the **75/25 (practical-deployment) pair** is the recommended compromise: ms ≈ 0.10 with violation below 5 % and energy close to the comfort-only end. We pre-registered 50/50 as canonical for symmetry, but the empirical 75/25 dominates it.
§9.  PI baseline reference
The BOPTEST built-in PI controller is included as a reproducible reference, not as a custom-tuned strong baseline. On the same 12-month yearly evaluation: mean comfort RMSE = 3.13 °C, mean MAE = 2.55 °C, mean violation = 60.7 %, mean monthly energy = 91.4 kWh, ms mean = 0.888. The PI baseline is **comfort-bad** (60 % violation is unacceptable) but **energy-good** (low kWh because it under-heats). The RL/MORL agents must beat PI on ms without dramatically exceeding PI energy use.
All Block 2 RL/MORL agents (pure v3 PPO, hybridl010, MORL 17D canonical) achieve ms well below 0.20 vs. PI's 0.89 — so the RL stack dominates the PI reference on comfort while using comparable or lower energy in absolute terms after correcting for the comfort/energy trade-off curve.
§10.  Transfer diagnostics — surrogate-vs-live closed-loop ms_gap
§10.1  Method
The transfer diagnostic closes a remaining evidence gap: when a policy is trained inside a surrogate-driven loop, does it transfer to the BOPTEST RTE without surprise? We compute, for each candidate backend, the m_s gap between the surrogate's predicted trajectory and the live-BOPTEST trajectory under the same policy and scenario, plus the first divergence step (the earliest 15-minute step at which the surrogate and BOPTEST diverge by more than a threshold), plus the top driver feature for the divergence.
Table 10 — Transfer diagnostics across three backend variants.  From reports/hybrid_transfer_comparison.csv. ms_gap = surrogate_m_s − live_m_s (negative means surrogate is optimistic).
§10.2  Findings
Direct_v35 has the largest ms_gap (|gap| ≈ 0.9–1.0) and the largest action-gap norm (2.0) — the surrogate-trained policy actually executes very different actions on the live RTE than it does in surrogate-rollout simulation. The driver feature is t_zone_norm, confirming that v3.5's sharper temperature dynamics produce policy actions that depend on small temperature differences not realised in live BOPTEST.
Pure_v3 has a moderate ms_gap (~0.07–0.10) driven by p_total_norm — the v3 surrogate under-estimates the live power draw, so the policy is mildly over-confident at deployment.
Hybrid_l010 has the smallest ms_gap (~0.02) and a larger first-divergence step on typical (step 16 vs. step 1 for the others) — meaning the policy stays close to its surrogate predictions for the first four hours before drifting. The disagreement regularizer has effectively narrowed the transfer gap.
§11.  Synthesis — fidelity-vs-utility paradox and Block 3 prerequisites
§11.1  The paradox restated
Block 2 establishes the central claim of the paper in its purest form. Predictive fidelity is necessary but not sufficient for control utility, and in the regime studied here it is also not sufficient by itself: the more accurate surrogate (v3.5, 24h RMSE 0.644 °C) produces a strictly worse PPO policy than the less accurate surrogate (v3, 24h RMSE 1.557 °C). The mechanism is gradient-quality, not bias: v3's smoother predictions yield lower-variance advantage estimates inside PPO.
§11.2  Cross-family generalisation
The same v3 + v3.5-as-soft-regularizer architecture works across three controller families:
Thermostatic PPO — best at lambda_temp_disagree = 0.10 (temperature censor on).
HDRL — best at lambda_temp_disagree = 0.00 (temperature censor off, power censor only).
MORL 17D — best at lambda_temp_disagree = 0.00 (same as HDRL); 17D observation is critical.
The pattern is: v3 is always the rollout dynamics; v3.5 is always a censor; what changes across controller families is which channels of the v3.5 prediction are used as censors. No single hybrid weight is optimal across families.
