Year-round operational optimization of HVAC systems using hierarchical
deep reinforcement learning for enhancing indoor air quality and reducing
energy consumption

HIGH LIGH TS

• Designed a hierarchical deep reinforcement learning (HDRL) control framework to address challenges in training with hybrid action spaces.
• Developed a simulation platform integrating heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) system and building energy models for reliable agent training and
  performance evaluation.
• Developed a comprehensive multi-objective reward function to balance indoor temperature, air quality, and energy efficiency.
• Achieved year-round operational performance improvements, broadening the applicability of DRL in HVAC control.

Keywords:                                                     Balancing user comfort demands with energy consumption in heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC)
Deep reinforcement learning                                   systems is a critical challenge in the field of control optimization. Currently, deep reinforcement learning (DRL)
HVAC system                                                   algorithms have achieved some success in optimizing HVAC system control. However, training agents with exist-
Year-round operational optimization                           ing algorithms becomes challenging, when dealing with the hybrid action control problem. This study proposes
Indoor environment quality
                                                              an HVAC system optimization control method based on hierarchical deep reinforcement learning (HDRL), which
Energy efficiency
                                                              aims to minimize system energy consumption while ensuring indoor comfort and maintaining air quality. The
                                                              proposed HDRL method employs a two-layer agent architecture, where the bottom agent is responsible for opti-
                                                              mizing the frequency control of fans and pumps and the top agent flexibly selects the daily operating mode based
                                                              on outdoor meteorological conditions. We designed a multi-objective reward function based on Gaussian distribu-
                                                              tion, hyperbolic tangent function, energy consumption normalization for bottom agents, and temperature-based
                                                              reward function for the top agent to achieve the reliable training of the agents. Experimental results indicated
                                                              that the HDRL method significantly improves indoor temperature and CO2 concentration control during the year-
                                                              round operation of the HVAC system. Compared to conventional PI control, the temperature compliance rate is
                                                              increased by 8.37 %, and the combined temperature and CO2 compliance rate is improved by 7.8 %, while
                                                              achieving a 3.95 % reduction in energy consumption.

                                                                                    larger state space complicates learning, and it is difficult to fully capture
                                                                                    operational data for all HVAC components. Considering these factors,

                                                                                        It is important to note that different state observations vary in units
                                                                                    and scales. We applied Min-Max normalization to scale all values be-

                                                                                    4.4. Design of the action space

                                                                                        The bottom agent controls the inverter frequencies of the system’s
                                                                                    fans and pumps. The action space is three-dimensional, and it consists
                                                                                    of continuous values for the frequency ratios of the AHU fan, OAU fan,
                                                                                    and pump (ranging from 0 to 1).
                                                                                        The top agent selects the optimal daily operating mode, with three
                                                                                    discrete values: Mode 1 (summer), Mode 2 (winter), and Mode 3 (tran-
                                                                                    sition). Then, the bottom agent executes specific inverter controls for
                                                                                    the selected mode. The hybrid nature of the action space makes training
                                                                                    challenging for traditional DRL algorithms: this is the primary reason to
                                                                                    adopt a hierarchical approach for training the agents.
