\subsection{Test case and runtime environment}\label{ssec:testcase}
The plant is the BOPTEST \texttt{bestest\_air} emulator~\cite{Blum2021BOPTEST,Wetter2014Modelica}, a
single-zone office of $6\times8\times2.7$~m (\SI{48}{\meter\squared}) whose envelope follows the
ASHRAE Standard~140 BESTEST Case~900 definition, with heavyweight concrete-block walls, an insulated
slab and \SI{12}{\meter\squared} of south glazing. Conditioning is an idealized four-pipe fan-coil
unit: a gas boiler of constant efficiency 0.9 feeds the heating coil and a chiller of constant
COP~3.0 the cooling coil, with a variable-speed supply fan. The zone is a two-person office occupied
08:00--18:00 with light internal gains and occupied comfort setpoints of 21/\SI{24}{\celsius}, under
Denver-Stapleton TMY weather.

Control is applied through the BOPTEST Run-Time Environment, a Dockerized Modelica emulator with
embedded baseline local-loop and supervisory controllers, a disturbance forecaster and a KPI
calculator, all exposed through a RESTful HTTP API~\cite{Arroyo2021GymBOPTEST}. The agent does not
replace the plant: it \emph{overwrites} one control input, the supply-air temperature setpoint,
while the remaining embedded loops run at baseline. At each \SI{900}{\second} control step it reads
measurements (zone temperature, CO$_2$, power) and disturbance forecasts, forms the observation,
writes the setpoint through the BOPTEST overwrite pair, and advances the emulator one step; the KPIs
are then computed by the runtime from the resulting live trajectory rather than from the surrogate.

The transfer study holds out three single-zone-like hydronic test cases with different heat sources,
distribution paths and actuator interfaces (Table~\ref{tab:testcases}). They form an
increasing-difficulty ladder away from the source case and share the same single-zone first-order
energy balance.

\subsection{Problem formulation and evaluation metrics}\label{ssec:pomdp}
Because the controller never observes the full latent state, control is a partially observable
Markov decision process. The agent receives an observation $o_t$ and selects
$a_t\sim\pi(\cdot\mid o_t)$ so as to maximize the expected discounted return~\cite{Sutton2018RL}
\begin{equation}
J(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^{t} r_t\right],
\qquad r_t=-\big(w_{\mathrm{c}} c_t + w_{\mathrm{e}} e_t\big),
\label{eq:return}
\end{equation}
with $c_t$ a thermal-discomfort term, $e_t$ the heating energy over the step, and
$w_{\mathrm{c}},w_{\mathrm{e}}$ fixed trade-off weights. This scalarized comfort--energy objective
follows the standard multi-objective treatment of HVAC control, where preferences are represented by
explicit weights rather than hidden inside a single black-box
reward~\cite{Roijers2013MOSurvey,Yang2019MORL,Hayes2022MOSurvey}. The comfort component is
piecewise-linear with an asymmetric, ambient-dependent slope, steeper for cold-weather undershoot
and fixed a priori from this physical asymmetry rather than tuned to any backend, plus a small
in-band bonus; the energy component is proportional to heating power.

Because HVAC control depends on forecasts, a purely instantaneous five-dimensional observation is
insufficient~\cite{Gao2024Predictive}: the canonical interface is a 17-dimensional observation
comprising physical states, cyclic time features, ambient forecasts and previous actions. The agent
acts through a single normalized supply-temperature command,
\begin{equation}
a_t\in[-1,1], \qquad T_{\mathrm{sup},t}=18+\tfrac{a_t+1}{2}\,(35-18)\quad[\si{\celsius}],
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
with a \SI{1.0}{\celsius} deadband, a per-step rate limit and a 21--\SI{24}{\celsius} comfort band.
The surrogate transition is \emph{trained} on a two-dimensional command, the supply-temperature
action together with a normalized fan setting, both excited only while the offline corpus is
generated; during every RL rollout and every live evaluation the fan is held at its BOPTEST baseline
and only the supply-temperature setpoint is commanded.

For evaluation, as opposed to training, deployed controllers are scored with the
duration-and-severity maintenance score of Wang et al.~\cite{Wang2025SafeDRL},
\begin{equation}
\ms = r_{\mathrm{time}} + r_{\mathrm{sev}},
\label{eq:ms}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\mathrm{time}}=N_{\mathrm{viol}}/N\in[0,1]$ is the fraction of control steps in which the
zone temperature leaves the comfort band $[T_{\mathrm{lo}},T_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ and
\begin{equation*}
r_{\mathrm{sev}}=\max_t\max\!\big((T_{\mathrm{zone},t}-T_{\mathrm{hi}})/T_{\mathrm{hi}},\,
(T_{\mathrm{lo}}-T_{\mathrm{zone},t})/T_{\mathrm{lo}},\,0\big)
\end{equation*}
is the largest single-step band exceedance normalized by the band bound. Both terms are
dimensionless and lower is better; because $r_{\mathrm{time}}\le 1$, the threshold $\ms>1$ flags a
controller that is either out of band for a large fraction of the horizon or commits a severe
worst-case excursion, the regime treated here as a failed deployment. Separating the shaped training
reward~\eqref{eq:return} from this unshaped safety score follows safe-RL
practice~\cite{Garcia2015SafeRL}. Predictive fidelity is reported as the multi-step rollout \RMSE{}
of the zone temperature; control utility as \ms{}, the comfort-violation fraction and heating
energy, always measured on the live emulator and never on the surrogate.

\subsection{Surrogate backends}\label{ssec:surrogates}
All surrogates approximate the same discrete-time transition $s_{t+1}=\Phi(s_t,a_t,d_t)$ and are
exposed behind a single adapter, so that only the internal dynamics change across comparisons, never
the control interface or the observation map. Each is fit to BOPTEST-generated transition tuples and
used as a \emph{deterministic point-transition} map rather than a learned conditional distribution,
which is appropriate where the zone's conditional dynamics are effectively deterministic given the
observed drivers, but does not represent transition uncertainty.

\paragraph{Control-oriented black-box surrogate (BB)}
BB is a compact black-box neural predictor (8482 parameters) of a temperature increment and a power
head, $\widehat{T}_{t+1}=T_t+f_{\theta,T}(x_t)$ and $\widehat{P}_{t+1}=f_{\theta,P}(x_t)$ with
$\widehat{P}\ge 0$, trained by a multi-horizon (1-, 2-, 4-step) supervised rollout objective with a
physical-consistency penalty. Its design goal is not long-horizon accuracy but a \emph{smooth,
well-conditioned} response surface for policy-gradient search. Its input vector is deliberately
low-dimensional: thermal state, outdoor disturbance, cyclic time embedding and the direct
supply-temperature action~\cite{Sun2024DirectTSup}. The canonical checkpoint is the early-stopped
optimum of a 215-epoch run with held-out one-step $R^2=0.991$. Its transition carries \emph{no}
explicit time step, so the increment learned on the canonical \SI{3600}{\second} corpus is applied
unchanged at the \SI{900}{\second} runtime step.

\paragraph{Physics-informed grey-box surrogate (GB)}
GB replaces the black box with a first-principles lumped-capacity zone balance whose unmodeled heat
flow is supplied by a neural residual head,
\begin{equation}
\Czon\,\frac{\mathrm{d}T_{\mathrm{zone}}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\dot{Q}(x;\phi),
\qquad \dot{Q}(x;\phi)=q_{\mathrm{scale}}\,q_\phi(x),
\label{eq:rc}
\end{equation}
with $q_{\mathrm{scale}}=\SI{3000}{\watt}$, integrated by a clipped explicit Euler step at the
control period, and with \Czon{} positively reparameterized and bounded below by
\SI{5e4}{\joule\per\kelvin}. Embedding this conservation law makes the model physics-informed in the
sense of~\cite{Raissi2019PINN,Karpatne2017TheoryGuided,Willard2022PIML,Hedayat2025PINN}, so \Czon{}
is physically interpretable, although the analysis treats it as an \emph{effective} capacitance that
may absorb unmodeled fast dynamics rather than a direct measurement of zone air capacitance. Because
\Czon{} is a property of the building rather than of the data-generating policy, it is expected to
remain meaningful across test cases, which is what makes the inverse identification below
worthwhile.

The canonical GB is calibrated by a staged inverse
identification~\cite{Bacher2011RCIdentification}: Stage~A aligns telemetry and removes latency and
bias; Stage~B identifies \Czon{} as a maximum-a-posteriori estimate under a weak Gaussian prior,
restricted to excitation-rich transients where the sensitivity $\partial\widehat{T}/\partial C$ is
large, since quasi-steady windows would merely pull the estimate back to the prior; and Stage~C
refines the neural residual heads with \Czon{} frozen. The identified
$\Czon=\SI{4.413e5}{\joule\per\kelvin}$ is a $+5.1\%$ update over the physical prior, within one
standard error of the Laplace/Fisher interval, and corresponds to about \SI{439}{\kilogram} of air,
the expected order for this zone; a supply-temperature sweep at fixed state gives the physically
correct sign in 100\% of 400 sampled states.

A third backend, matched-resolution BB, is the \emph{same} black-box architecture retrained on a
\SI{900}{\second} corpus. It exists solely to isolate the model-class confound in
Section~\ref{sec:paradox}. Retraining on that corpus turns out to break the model's control channel
(Section~\ref{sec:fidelity}), so the backend is carried in two forms: as trained, and with a
monotonicity constraint that restores directional validity. The constraint penalizes, at jittered
states, any predicted temperature decrease in response to an increased supply-temperature command;
it touches no other part of the recipe.

\subsection{Role-separated hybrid training environment}\label{ssec:hybrid}
The hybrid backend is not a new RL algorithm. It is an application-specific role-separation strategy
grounded in three existing lines of work: model-based and world-model training in learned
environments~\cite{Sutton1991Dyna,Hafner2025Dreamer}, physics-informed and grey-box surrogate
modeling~\cite{Bacher2011RCIdentification,Raissi2019PINN,Willard2022PIML}, and safe-RL reward
shaping~\cite{Garcia2015SafeRL,Wang2025SafeDRL}. The contribution is the allocation of roles inside
surrogate-trained HVAC control (Fig.~\ref{fig:hybrid}): BB supplies the smooth rollout dynamics,
while a \emph{frozen} GB acts only as a per-step reward-shaping censor. Training uses the shaped
reward
\begin{equation}
\tilde{r}_t=r_t-\lambda_T\,\delta_{T,t}-\lambda_P\,\delta_{P,t},
\label{eq:hybrid}
\end{equation}
where the disagreement terms are the per-step $L_1$ distances between the one-step predictions of
the rollout surrogate $f_\theta$ (BB) and the frozen twin $g_\phi$ (GB), separately for the
zone-temperature and HVAC power channels,
\begin{equation*}
\delta_{\bullet,t}=\big|f_{\theta,\bullet}(s_t,a_t,d_t)-g_{\phi,\bullet}(s_t,a_t,d_t)\big|,
\end{equation*}
with no squaring, normalization or clipping. The two censor weights differ in scale because they
multiply a temperature residual in \si{\celsius} and a power residual in \si{\watt}; for the
thermostatic controller $\lambda_T=0.10$ and $\lambda_P=5\times10^{-5}$.

Crucially, $g_\phi$ is evaluated \emph{forward only} and its outputs are detached, so the censor
enters the \emph{reward} alone, never the policy loss, and never the rollout dynamics, which remain
those of BB. Because a policy-gradient method estimates its gradient from sampled returns rather
than by differentiating the environment, this role split does not preserve any
environment-to-policy gradient; what it preserves is the smooth trajectory-and-return distribution
of BB, and its favorable optimization conditioning, while penalizing state--action regions on which
the frozen twin disagrees with the rollout. In other words, GB does not replace BB, form an
accuracy-oriented ensemble, or introduce a second policy-loss term. The penalty measures
disagreement between two imperfect surrogates, not violation of a conservation law or of a
physically feasible set; it is therefore a \emph{model-disagreement} censor, physics-informed
through GB's structure but not a hard physical-feasibility filter.

\subsection{Controller families and protocol}\label{ssec:protocol}
All policies are optimized with proximal policy optimization (PPO)~\cite{Schulman2017PPO} using
generalized advantage estimation~\cite{Schulman2016GAE} and the Stable-Baselines3
implementation~\cite{Raffin2021SB3}. Shared settings are learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, discount
factor $\gamma=0.99$ (an effective horizon of about \SI{25}{\hour} at the \SI{900}{\second} step)
and ten optimization epochs per rollout; the families differ in rollout length, minibatch size and
total timestep budget. The recipe is evaluated across three controller families of increasing
structure: a \emph{thermostatic} PPO agent on the shared forecast-augmented observation; a
\emph{hierarchical} DRL controller (HDRL) that decomposes control across timescales in the
options/feudal tradition~\cite{Bacon2017Options,Nachum2018HIRO} as recently applied to year-round
HVAC operation~\cite{Liao2025HDRL}; and a \emph{preference-conditioned} multi-objective controller
(MORL)~\cite{Yang2019MORL,Byeon2025MaxMinMORL}.

Policies are trained on their surrogate backend and then evaluated \emph{zero-shot} in closed loop
on the live emulator over two targeted 14-day windows: a January peak-heat window with daily-mean
ambient \SI{-24.4}{\celsius} and a February typical-heat window at \SI{+2.4}{\celsius}. Because a
policy trained on a surrogate is deployed on a different, live system, the central methodological
risk is distribution shift~\cite{Quinonero2009DatasetShift}; the evaluation therefore always reports
the live-emulator score. Each hypothesis, pass threshold and recalibration adapter was pre-specified
\emph{before} the corresponding runs.

\paragraph{Implementation}
Surrogates use three corpora generated by scripted excitation: a canonical BB corpus (51\,200 rows
at \SI{1}{\hour}), a 15-minute exploration corpus (48\,384 rows at \SI{900}{\second}) and a GB
calibration corpus (10\,744 rows at \SI{900}{\second}); the deliberate one-hour/fifteen-minute
pairing is the instrument behind the matched-corpus ablation. All controllers are trained in
Stable-Baselines3~2.1.0 under Python~3.11.9. Experiments ran on a laptop workstation (6-core CPU,
32~GB RAM, laptop-class GPU) with the emulator in a Docker container. Behind one HTTP contract sit
four interchangeable backends (live BOPTEST, BB, GB, hybrid), so only the dynamics backend changes
across the surrogate-versus-live comparison; the surrogate backends run \numrange{85.0}{220.2} times
faster than the live loop, so a $5\times10^6$-step policy-optimization run costs about 47 minutes of
environment time against roughly 66 hours on the live runtime. Canonical KPI tables use seed~42; the
four central backends additionally carry an $N=3$ seed-robustness audit over seeds
$\{42,43,44\}$, MORL is evaluated over five fixed seeds, and the HDRL censor-weight sweep is replicated over the
same three seeds at each of its four $\lambda_T$ settings.
