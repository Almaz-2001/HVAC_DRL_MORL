\abstract{Deep reinforcement-learning (RL) controllers for HVAC are often trained against fast neural-network surrogates, under the assumption that a more accurate surrogate provides a better training environment. We test this assumption on the BOPTEST \texttt{bestest\_air} testcase and report a negative result, the \emph{fidelity--utility paradox}: the surrogate with lower predictive error can be the worse environment for policy-gradient search. A grey-box RC surrogate with a neural residual heat-flow head (GB) attains a 24-hour rollout RMSE of $0.644\,^{\circ}$C versus $1.557\,^{\circ}$C for a black-box surrogate (BB), yet a policy trained directly on GB collapses on every seed: its BOPTEST maintenance score is $m_s = 1.07$/$1.13$ peak/typical (lower is better; $m_s > 1$ is unusable, $N{=}3$), with comfort violation $>75\%$, whereas the weaker BB trains a usable controller ($m_s = 0.073$/$0.095$). Retraining BB at finer resolution makes it \emph{more} accurate yet unusable ($m_s > 1.1$), showing that the driver is temporal resolution and the coarse per-step increment, not model class alone. We then formulate a role-separated hybrid methodology: BB supplies smooth rollout dynamics, while a frozen GB acts only as a per-step model-disagreement reward censor. This role separation gives the best cross-window robustness ($m_s = 0.041$, sub-$5\%$ violation) at ${\sim}85\times$ live-simulator throughput, with a controller-family-specific censor weight. Across three hydronic transfer cases, recalibration transfers but the frozen controller does not, indicating a component-level rather than universal transfer boundary.}
