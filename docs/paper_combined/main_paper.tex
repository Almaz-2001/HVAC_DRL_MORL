\begin{abstract}
Deep reinforcement-learning (RL) controllers for HVAC are often trained against fast neural-network surrogates, under the assumption that a more accurate surrogate provides a better training environment. This study tests that assumption on the BOPTEST \texttt{bestest\_air} testcase and reports a negative result, the \emph{fidelity--utility paradox}: the surrogate with lower predictive error can be the worse environment for policy-gradient search. A grey-box resistance--capacitance surrogate with a neural residual heat-flow head (GB) attains a 24-hour rollout RMSE of $0.644\,^{\circ}$C versus $1.557\,^{\circ}$C for a black-box surrogate (BB), yet a policy trained directly on GB collapses on every seed: its BOPTEST maintenance score is $m_s = 1.07$/$1.13$ peak/typical ($N{=}3$ seed mean; lower is better, $m_s > 1$ is unusable), with comfort violation $>75\%$, whereas the weaker BB trains a usable controller (canonical seed-42 $m_s = 0.073$/$0.095$). Retraining the black-box surrogate at finer resolution makes it \emph{more} accurate yet unusable for training ($m_s > 1.1$), showing that the driver is temporal resolution and the coarse per-step increment, not model class alone. We then formulate a role-separated hybrid methodology: BB supplies smooth rollout dynamics, while a frozen GB acts only as a per-step model-disagreement reward censor. This role separation gives the best cross-window robustness ($m_s = 0.041$, sub-$5\%$ comfort violation on both windows) at ${\sim}85\times$ live-simulator throughput, with a controller-family-specific censor weight. Across three hydronic transfer cases, recalibration transfers but the frozen controller does not, indicating a component-level rather than universal transfer boundary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Buildings account for roughly one third of global final energy use, and heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning (HVAC) systems are their single largest consumer~\citep{AlSayed2024Review,Nguyen2024Modelling}, making HVAC control a high-leverage decarbonization target. It is also intrinsically hard: zone thermodynamics are nonlinear, coupled, and driven by time-varying occupancy and weather. The proportional--integral (PI) and rule-based controllers used in practice are robust but cannot exploit this structure~\citep{Savino2025ASHRAE,Gao2024Predictive}, whereas model predictive control can, in principle, but needs an accurate, continuously maintained building model that is expensive to identify and brittle under mismatch~\citep{Hou2024MultiSource}.

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) is an attractive alternative because it learns a control policy directly from interaction, without an explicit model, and standardized runtime environments such as BOPTEST now make such controllers reproducibly comparable~\citep{Blum2021BOPTEST,AlSayed2024Review}. The obstacle is the sim-to-real deployment gap: policy-gradient algorithms consume millions of environment steps, so training on a live building, or on a high-fidelity physics simulator stepped through an HTTP interface, is prohibitively slow and risks sustained comfort violations during exploration~\citep{Wang2025SafeDRL,Hou2024MultiSource}. The community's response is to train the policy against a fast neural-network surrogate of the building, increasingly a physics-informed digital twin (e.g.\ resistance--capacitance or Neural Ordinary Differential Equation models) that generates state transitions orders of magnitude faster than real time~\citep{HouEvins2024,Mshragi2026FastML}. This paradigm proceeds on a rarely examined assumption, which this study makes explicit and tests: that a surrogate with higher predictive fidelity is automatically a better environment in which to train a controller.

We find this assumption can fail, exposing the \emph{fidelity--utility paradox} (Fig.~\ref{fig:system_overview}): the surrogate with the lowest multi-step rollout error can be the worst environment for policy-gradient search. Trained directly on the high-fidelity physical twin, the policy converges to a near bang-bang law and fails on the live BOPTEST runtime ($m_s>1$, comfort violation above $77\%$), even though that twin is the more accurate offline predictor. The driver is measurable on the surrogate side: the higher-fidelity backends expose a much rougher action$\to$next-temperature response surface, which policy-gradient search exploits into a policy that does not survive distribution shift to the live simulator~\citep{RiahiSamani2026OOD}; Section~\ref{sec:results2-control} isolates the coarse per-step increment magnitude as the operative property. The methodological point is that predictive fidelity, corpus resolution, and control utility must be separated by controlled decomposition, not compared as a single RMSE number.

Two secondary gaps accompany the central one. HVAC control is partially observable: a small ($5$-dimensional) instantaneous observation is too impoverished for forecast-dependent and preference-conditioned control~\citep{Byeon2025MaxMinMORL,Gao2024Predictive}, and a single physics-regularization strength need not suit thermostatic, hierarchical, and multi-objective agents alike. Existing studies seldom separate surrogate fidelity from controller utility, rarely scrutinize the observation interface, and almost never pre-specify cross-testcase transfer, the methodological gap addressed here.

The proposed method is a hybrid, role-separating architecture that decouples prediction from training (Fig.~\ref{fig:system_overview}). A compact control-oriented black-box surrogate (BB) supplies smooth, control-friendly Markov rollout dynamics, while an inverse-calibrated physics-informed twin (GB), whose zone thermal capacitance $\Czon$ is identified from telemetry, acts as a \emph{frozen per-step reward-shaping censor}. The censor adds a disagreement penalty to the reward, not a term to the policy loss, and the frozen GB never becomes the rollout model, so the policy keeps learning over BB's smooth rollout dynamics while state--action regions on which the physics-informed twin disagrees with the rollout surrogate are discouraged. To address partial observability, the controller interface is widened to a $17$-dimensional, forecast-augmented direct-supply-temperature observation. The recipe is evaluated across three controller families: thermostatic proximal policy optimization (PPO), hierarchical DRL (HDRL)~\citep{Liao2025HDRL}, and preference-conditioned MORL~\citep{Byeon2025MaxMinMORL}; the resulting frozen policies are then subjected to a pre-specified transferability study on a hydronic family of BOPTEST testcases.

The evidence hierarchy is deliberately narrow. The primary causal test is the four-backend thermostatic-PPO comparison: coarse BB, matched-resolution BB, direct GB, and the hybrid BB/GB backend. HDRL, MORL, and hydronic transfer are not used to enlarge that central claim; they are boundary tests that identify where the role-separated recipe does and does not transfer. This separation prevents predictive fidelity, training utility, controller-family regularization, and cross-testcase transfer from being interpreted as one undifferentiated result.

We separate four questions that are often conflated, stated as pre-specified, falsifiable claims (H1--H4) and reported whether supported or falsified; H1--H2 are the primary paradox and its remedy, while H3--H4 are boundary tests that map the recipe's scope:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{H\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.6em]
 \item \emph{Fidelity--utility:} a surrogate with higher predictive fidelity is a better RL training environment.
 \item \emph{Role separation:} using the physical twin as a frozen reward-shaping censor over a smooth black-box rollout recovers the control utility that direct use of the twin destroys.
 \item \emph{Censor-weight universality:} a single model-disagreement censor weight is sufficient (transfers) across controller families. (Falsification of this hypothesis is what establishes controller-family specificity.)
 \item \emph{Transferability:} the inverse-calibration pipeline and the frozen controller transfer to related hydronic testcases under documented recalibration.
\end{enumerate}
Stating these in advance lets the negative outcomes be read as findings rather than tuning artefacts: direct-GB collapse, censor-weight non-universality (H3 falsified), and regime-dependent policy transfer. In particular, the calibration pipeline transfers more reliably than the frozen controller, the component-level boundary identified by the study.

The study makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Predictive fidelity is separated from RL training utility.} A calibrated GB twin is the best predictor, but direct PPO training on it collapses in live BOPTEST. A matched-resolution black-box BB ablation shows that the failure is not a grey-box artefact: increasing temporal fidelity can also destroy control utility.
 \item \textbf{The collapse is diagnosed and repaired by role separation.} The collapsing single-model backends expose rougher action-response surfaces and produce near bang-bang policies. The hybrid backend keeps BB as the smooth rollout model and uses frozen GB only as a reward-shaping censor.
 \item \textbf{The transfer boundary is component-level.} The calibration pipeline transfers across hydronic testcases, but frozen-policy transfer is regime-dependent. HDRL and MORL further show that the model-disagreement censor weight is controller-family specific.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
Reproducible HVAC-control research depends on a shared benchmark, because controller claims otherwise vary with the simulator, weather year, disturbance schedule, and comfort definition. BOPTEST serves Modelica building models through a uniform API \citep{Blum2021BOPTEST,Wetter2014Modelica} with a Gym interface for RL workflows \citep{Arroyo2021GymBOPTEST}, complementing demand-response benchmarks such as CityLearn \citep{VazquezCanteli2019CityLearn,VazquezCanteli2019Review}; every controller here is finally re-scored in BOPTEST, not only in its learned environment. Against this benchmark, HVAC DRL studies compare learned policies with rule-based, Guideline~36, or MPC references \citep{Savino2025ASHRAE,Wang2025SafeDRL,Nguyen2024Modelling,Sun2024DirectTSup} and extend them to multi-agent and context-aware settings \citep{Deng2025MultiAgent,Alotaibi2025ContextAware}; hierarchical policies decompose comfort/energy objectives across timescales \citep{Liao2025HDRL,Bacon2017Options,Nachum2018HIRO}, and MORL represents the energy--comfort trade-off explicitly \citep{Yang2019MORL,Roijers2013MOSurvey,Hayes2022MOSurvey,Byeon2025MaxMinMORL}. These works fix the training environment, which is precisely what this study varies.

Learned building surrogates make DRL-scale training feasible where direct simulation is too slow, spanning black-box networks, grey-box RC models, Neural ODEs, and physics-informed learning \citep{Bacher2011RCIdentification,Berthou2014RC,Bunning2020RC,Picard2017WhiteBox,Raissi2019PINN,Chen2018NeuralODE,Karpatne2017TheoryGuided,Willard2022PIML,Hedayat2025PINN}, and connecting to the broader scientific-machine-learning surrogate literature on operator learning and projection-based reduced-order models \citep{Lu2021DeepONet,Li2021FNO,Benner2015ROM}; \citet{HouEvins2024} frame surrogate development as a staged validation protocol and fast-surrogate work emphasises runtime feasibility \citep{Mshragi2026FastML}. Most of this literature validates prediction metrics, yet a controller (like a surrogate-based optimizer that can drive an accurate surrogate into infeasible optima by exploiting its residual error \citep{Jones1998EGO}) uses the surrogate as a closed-loop transition function under policy-induced states, so we report predictive fidelity and runtime first, then test the stronger claim that fidelity implies training utility.

That stronger claim is a distribution-shift question. On-policy data collection and observation design shape the states an RL controller learns on \citep{Mayor2025Parallelized,Radac2025OnlineRL,Gao2024Predictive}, and safe-RL work shows that small model errors become operationally important once actions are optimised against them \citep{Garcia2015SafeRL,Wang2025SafeDRL}. This links HVAC control to model exploitation and dataset shift in model-based RL \citep{Quinonero2009DatasetShift,RiahiSamani2026OOD}, and specifically to the \emph{objective mismatch} between a surrogate trained for predictive likelihood and one that yields a good downstream policy \citep{Lambert2020ObjectiveMismatch,Janner2019MBPO}; decision- and value-aware model learning answer this mismatch by retraining the model toward the control objective \citep{Farahmand2017VAML}, and the same low-error-yet-poor-transfer effect is central to sim-to-real deployment \citep{Zhao2020Sim2Real}. A low test-set RMSE thus does not guarantee correct behaviour on policy-induced states, and our negative direct-GB result is a control-oriented instance of this mismatch. Unlike value-aware retraining, we hold both surrogates fixed and instead \emph{measure} the mismatch mechanism, action-response roughness induced by temporal resolution, and resolve it by \emph{role separation} rather than by changing the surrogate's training objective.

Finally, transfer-oriented HVAC work reduces the cost of adapting DRL controllers across buildings, zones, and climates \citep{Hou2024MultiSource,Coraci2024HeteroTL,Coraci2025HiLo,Meng2025Lifelong}, and world-model/Dyna control motivates learning in an internal model before deployment \citep{Sutton1991Dyna,Hafner2025Dreamer}. These lines rarely separate two questions we keep distinct: whether the surrogate recalibrates to the target physics, and whether a frozen controller stays deployable through a new actuator interface, which our version-locked hydronic transfer study answers separately.

\subsection{BOPTEST task and evaluation metric}\label{ssec:m-env}\label{ssec:m-pomdp}
The plant is a single-zone building emulator with heating and cooling actuation, drawn from the
BOPTEST family of Modelica-based test cases \citep{Blum2021BOPTEST,Wetter2014Modelica}
and served to the controller through BOPTEST's uniform HTTP interface
\citep{Arroyo2021GymBOPTEST}. Formally the emulator is a continuous-time
dynamical system whose latent thermal state evolves under a scalar control action
$a_t\in[-1,1]$ (mapped to a supply-temperature set-point by Eq.~\eqref{eq:action_map}) and an exogenous disturbance stream $d_t$ (ambient weather,
solar gain, occupancy, and time-varying comfort set-points). The environment is queried
at a fixed control period $\Delta t = \SI{900}{\second}$ (15 minutes), yielding the
discrete-time transition $s_{t+1}=\Phi(s_t,a_t,d_t)$ that all surrogates are trained
to approximate. The primary test case is \texttt{bestest\_air}; a hydronic family of
related test cases is held out for the transferability study (Section~\ref{sec:results3-transfer}).

\paragraph{Primary test case.} \texttt{bestest\_air} is a single-zone office of $6\times8\times2.7$\,m ($48$\,m$^2$) whose envelope follows the ASHRAE~Standard~140 BESTEST Case~900 definition (heavyweight concrete-block walls, an insulated slab, and $12$\,m$^2$ of south glazing). Conditioning is an idealized four-pipe fan-coil unit: a gas boiler (constant efficiency $0.9$) feeds the heating coil and a chiller (constant COP $3.0$) the cooling coil, with a variable-speed supply fan. The zone is a two-person office occupied 08:00--18:00 with light internal gains and occupied comfort set-points $21$/$24\,^\circ$C, under Denver-Stapleton (CO, USA) TMY weather. Of the available BOPTEST actuators, the controller commands only the supply-air-temperature set-point, which the action map~\eqref{eq:action_map} restricts to $18$--$35\,^\circ$C, and observes zone temperature, CO$_2$, and power.

\paragraph{Transfer family.} The transfer set contains three single-zone-like hydronic BOPTEST cases with different heat sources, distribution paths, and actuator interfaces (Table~\ref{tab:testcases}); they form an increasing-difficulty ladder away from \texttt{bestest\_air}, share the single-zone first-order energy balance~\eqref{eq:hydronic_balance}, and are bridged by the per-test-case adapters of Section~\ref{sec:results3-transfer} (per-case schematics in Supplementary Figure~\suppref{fig:testcases}).

\paragraph{Problem formulation.}
Because the controller never observes the full latent state, control is a partially
observable Markov decision process (POMDP)
$\langle \mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O},\Phi,R,\gamma\rangle$, where the agent
receives an observation $o_t=h(s_t,d_t)\in\mathcal{O}$ and selects $a_t\sim\pi(\cdot\mid o_t)$
so as to maximise the expected discounted return~\citep{Sutton2018RL}
\begin{equation}
J(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^{t}\,r_t\right],
\qquad
r_t = -\big(w_{\mathrm{c}}\,c_t + w_{\mathrm{e}}\,e_t\big),
\label{eq:m-return}
\end{equation}
with $c_t$ a thermal-discomfort term, $e_t$ the heating energy over the step, and
$w_{\mathrm{c}},w_{\mathrm{e}}$ trade-off weights. This scalarized comfort--energy objective follows the standard multi-objective RL treatment of HVAC control, where preferences are represented by explicit weights rather than hidden inside a single black-box reward \citep{Roijers2013MOSurvey,Yang2019MORL,Hayes2022MOSurvey}. The comfort component is piecewise-linear with an asymmetric, ambient-dependent slope (steeper penalties for cold-weather undershoot, fixed a priori from this physical asymmetry rather than tuned to any backend), plus a small in-band bonus, and the energy component is proportional to heating power; the full functional form and its fixed parameters are given in the Supplementary Material. Because HVAC control is partially observable, a five-dimensional instantaneous observation is insufficient and a forecast-augmented one is required \citep{Gao2024Predictive}: the canonical interface is a 17-dimensional observation (physical states, cyclic time features, ambient forecasts, and previous actions; full per-feature breakdown in Supplementary Table~\suppref{tab:obs17}), whereas the failed MORL baseline uses only the earlier 5D observation. The agent acts through a single normalized supply-temperature command,
\begin{equation}
 a_t \in [-1,1],
 \qquad
 T_{\mathrm{sup},t} = 18 + \tfrac{a_t+1}{2}(35-18)\quad [^\circ\mathrm{C}],
 \label{eq:action_map}
\end{equation}
with a $1.0\,^\circ$C deadband, a per-step rate limit, and a $21$--$24\,^\circ$C comfort band. The controller's action space is one-dimensional throughout: the policy emits only this scalar supply-temperature command. The surrogate transition is \emph{trained} on a two-dimensional command $(a_0,a_1)$ (the supply-temperature action $a_t$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:action_map} together with a normalized fan setting $a_1$, both excited only while the offline corpus is generated), but during every RL rollout and every live BOPTEST evaluation the fan is held at its BOPTEST baseline ($a_1$ fixed) and only the supply-temperature set-point is commanded, which is also the single input that BOPTEST overwrites (Section~\ref{ssec:m-eval}). For
\emph{evaluation} (as opposed to training) deployed controllers are scored with the
duration-and-severity maintenance score
\begin{equation}
m_s = r_{\mathrm{time}} + r_{\mathrm{sev}},
\label{eq:m-ms}
\end{equation}
the time-in-violation plus worst-case relative-severity metric of \citet{Wang2025SafeDRL};
lower is better and $m_s>1$ marks an unacceptable controller. Explicitly,
$r_{\mathrm{time}}=N_{\mathrm{viol}}/N\in[0,1]$ is the fraction of control steps in which the
zone temperature leaves the comfort band $[T_{\mathrm{lo}},T_{\mathrm{hi}}]$, and
$r_{\mathrm{sev}}=\max_t\max\!\big((T_{\mathrm{zone},t}-T_{\mathrm{hi}})/T_{\mathrm{hi}},\,
(T_{\mathrm{lo}}-T_{\mathrm{zone},t})/T_{\mathrm{lo}},\,0\big)$ is the largest single-step band
exceedance normalised by the band bound; both terms are dimensionless. Because
$r_{\mathrm{time}}\le 1$, the threshold $m_s>1$ flags a controller that is either out of band for
a large fraction of the horizon or commits a severe worst-case excursion, the regime treated
here as a failed deployment. Because $r_{\mathrm{sev}}$ normalises the excursion by the Celsius band bound, the $m_s>1$ cut is a reporting convention inherited from \citet{Wang2025SafeDRL} rather than a physically invariant boundary. This separation of a
shaped training reward~\eqref{eq:m-return} from an unshaped safety score~\eqref{eq:m-ms}
follows safe-RL evaluation practice \citep{Garcia2015SafeRL} and the HVAC safety-score construction used by \citet{Wang2025SafeDRL}. Thus $m_s$ is not introduced as a new physical quantity; it is an operational maintenance score adapted from prior HVAC DRL evaluation work and implemented consistently across all BOPTEST runs.

\subsection{Surrogate backends and the paradox test}\label{ssec:m-surrogate}
All three surrogates are fast approximations of $\Phi$ used as the training
environment in place of the slow emulator, in the model-based / Dyna tradition of
learning a model and planning or training a policy against it
\citep{Sutton1991Dyna,Hafner2025Dreamer}. Each is fit to BOPTEST-generated
transition tuples $(s_t,a_t,d_t,s_{t+1})$ and used as a \emph{deterministic
point-transition} map: during every RL rollout the next state is set to
$\hat s_{t+1}=f_\theta(s_t,a_t,d_t)$ (or its GB analogue $g_\phi$), not sampled from
a learned conditional distribution $P_\theta(\cdot\mid s_t,a_t,d_t)$. This is
appropriate where the zone's conditional dynamics are effectively deterministic
given the observed drivers; it does not represent transition uncertainty, a
recognised limitation for the noisier power channel (Section~\ref{ssec:b3lim}). The
full field-by-field transition-dataset schema (tagging each field as a BOPTEST
measurement, an exogenous disturbance, a derived feature, a control action, or a
regression target, and giving the recursive-rollout state update) is provided in
the Supplementary Material. They differ in how much building physics is hard-wired
into their structure.

\paragraph{Control-oriented surrogate BB.}
BB is a compact black-box neural one-step predictor
$\hat{s}_{t+1}=f_\theta(s_t,a_t,d_t)$, trained by supervised minimisation of a
multi-step rollout error so that long-horizon trajectories, not just one-step
residuals, stay accurate. Its design goal is not maximal predictive accuracy but
a \emph{smooth, well-conditioned} response surface for policy-gradient search; the
architecture and parameter count are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results1-digital-twin}.

\paragraph{Physically informed surrogate GB and Stage A/B/C calibration.}
GB replaces the black box with a grey-box resistance--capacitance (RC) thermal
network whose unmodelled heat flow is supplied by a neural residual head. It is an
ODE-structured grey-box surrogate, a continuous-time RC balance, in the spirit
of Neural ODEs \citep{Chen2018NeuralODE} and classical RC building identification
\citep{Bunning2020RC,Berthou2014RC,Bacher2011RCIdentification,Picard2017WhiteBox},
but integrated discretely by an explicit Euler step at the control period
$\Delta t$ rather than by an adaptive ODE solver. Writing the zone capacitance as
$\Czon$ and a lumped envelope resistance as $R$, the core balance is
\begin{equation*}
\Czon\,\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{\mathrm{d}t}
= \frac{T_{\mathrm{amb}}-T}{R} + \eta\,a_t\,\dot{Q}_{\max} + q_d,
\end{equation*}
with $\eta\,a_t\,\dot{Q}_{\max}$ the delivered heating power and $q_d$ aggregated
disturbance gains (the neural residual head). Embedding this conservation law in the
network makes it physics-informed in the sense of
\citet{Raissi2019PINN,Karpatne2017TheoryGuided,Willard2022PIML}, so its parameters
are physically interpretable; the analysis nonetheless treats $\Czon$ as an \emph{effective}
thermal capacitance under the chosen surrogate structure, a lumped parameter
that may absorb unmodelled fast dynamics, rather than a direct measurement of
zone air capacitance. The parameters are recovered by a staged inverse calibration
(Stages~A/B/C) in the spirit of staged grey-box calibration
\citep{Bacher2011RCIdentification}, and the identifiability of $\Czon$ is checked
with a Fisher/Laplace confidence interval; the staged protocol and the resulting
calibrated values are detailed in Section~\ref{sec:results1-digital-twin}.


\subsection{Role-separated hybrid remedy}\label{ssec:m-hybrid}
The hybrid backend is not introduced as a new reinforcement-learning algorithm.
It is an application-specific role-separation strategy grounded in three existing
lines of work: model-based/Dyna and world-model training in learned environments
\citep{Sutton1991Dyna,Hafner2025Dreamer}, physics-informed and grey-box surrogate
modelling \citep{Bacher2011RCIdentification,Raissi2019PINN,Karpatne2017TheoryGuided,Willard2022PIML},
and safe-RL reward shaping or constraint penalties \citep{Garcia2015SafeRL,Wang2025SafeDRL}.
The contribution here is the allocation of roles inside surrogate-trained HVAC
control: BB supplies the smooth rollout dynamics, while a \emph{frozen} GB acts
only as a per-step reward-shaping censor. Training uses the shaped reward
\begin{equation}
\tilde{r}_t = r_t - \lambda_T\,\delta_{T,t} - \lambda_P\,\delta_{P,t},
\label{eq:m-hybrid}
\end{equation}
where the disagreement terms are the per-step $L_1$ (absolute) distances between the
one-step predictions of the rollout surrogate $f_\theta$ (BB) and the frozen twin
$g_\phi$ (GB), separately for the zone temperature and the HVAC power channel,
\begin{equation*}
\delta_{T,t} = \big|\, f_{\theta,T}(s_t,a_t,d_t) - g_{\phi,T}(s_t,a_t,d_t)\,\big|\ [\si{\celsius}],
\qquad
\delta_{P,t} = \big|\, f_{\theta,P}(s_t,a_t,d_t) - g_{\phi,P}(s_t,a_t,d_t)\,\big|\ [\si{\watt}],
\end{equation*}
with no squaring, normalisation, or clipping. The two censor weights differ in scale
because they multiply a temperature residual (in \si{\celsius}) and a power residual
(in \si{\watt}); for the thermostatic controller $\lambda_T = 0.10$ and
$\lambda_P = 5\times10^{-5}$. Crucially, $g_\phi$ is evaluated \emph{forward only}
and its outputs are detached, so the censor enters the \emph{reward} alone, never
the policy loss, and never the rollout dynamics, which remain those of BB. PPO
estimates its policy gradient from sampled returns rather than by differentiating
the environment, so this role split does not preserve any environment-to-policy
gradient; what it preserves is the smooth BB \emph{trajectory-and-return
distribution} of Section~\ref{ssec:m-surrogate} (and its favourable optimisation
conditioning), while penalising state--action regions on which the frozen twin
disagrees with the rollout. In other words, GB does not replace BB, form an
accuracy-oriented ensemble, or introduce a second policy-loss term. The penalty
measures disagreement between two imperfect surrogates, not violation of a
conservation law or a physically feasible set; it is therefore a
\emph{model-disagreement} censor (physics-informed through GB's structure, but not
a hard physical-feasibility filter), applied forward-only over the smoother BB
training environment (Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid_mechanism}). The swept values of $\lambda_T$ per controller family are given in
Section~\ref{sec:results2-control}.

\paragraph{Controller families.}\label{ssec:m-controllers}
All policies are optimised with proximal policy optimization (PPO)
\citep{Schulman2017PPO} using generalized advantage estimation
\citep{Schulman2016GAE} and the Stable-Baselines3 implementation
\citep{Raffin2021SB3}; PPO maximises the standard clipped surrogate objective with generalized-advantage estimates (full form in the Supplementary Material). The shared PPO settings are learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, discount factor $\gamma=0.99$ (an effective horizon of ${\sim}25$\,h at the $900$\,s step), and ten optimization epochs per rollout; the families differ in rollout length, minibatch size, and total timestep budget. The surrogate recipe is evaluated across three
controller families of increasing structure: (i) a \emph{thermostatic} PPO agent on
the shared forecast-augmented observation; (ii) a \emph{hierarchical} DRL controller (HDRL) that
decomposes control across timescales, in the options/feudal tradition
\citep{Bacon2017Options,Nachum2018HIRO} as recently applied to year-round HVAC
operation \citep{Liao2025HDRL}; and (iii) a \emph{preference-conditioned}
multi-objective controller (MORL) that conditions on a comfort--energy preference
vector \citep{Yang2019MORL,Roijers2013MOSurvey,Hayes2022MOSurvey,Byeon2025MaxMinMORL}
and acts on the forecast-augmented observation. All families share the 17D forecast-augmented observation and supply-temperature action~\eqref{eq:action_map} of Section~\ref{ssec:m-pomdp}; the failed MORL baseline instead uses a 5D instantaneous observation.

\subsection{Evaluation and transfer protocol}\label{ssec:m-eval}
Control is applied to the live plant through the BOPTEST Run-Time Environment (RTE): a Dockerised Modelica emulator with embedded baseline (local-loop and supervisory) controllers, a disturbance forecaster, and a KPI calculator, all exposed through a RESTful HTTP API \citep{Blum2021BOPTEST}. The agent does not replace the plant; it \emph{overwrites} one control input, the supply-temperature set-point, while the remaining embedded loops run at baseline. At each \SI{900}{\second} control step it reads measurements $y_t$ (zone temperature, CO$_2$, power) and disturbance forecasts $\omega_t$, forms the 17D observation, maps its action to a supply temperature through~\eqref{eq:action_map}, writes that set-point through the BOPTEST overwrite pair, and advances the emulator one step; the maintenance score and other KPIs are then computed by the RTE from the resulting live trajectory rather than from the surrogate. The hydronic transfer cases reuse the same supply-temperature command, routed through the per-test-case adapters of Section~\ref{sec:results3-transfer}. Figure~\suppref{fig:rte_loop} details the loop and the overwrite-signal mechanism.

Surrogate predictive fidelity is reported with the ASHRAE-style calibration
statistics CV(\RMSE) and NMBE over multi-step rollouts, the standard accuracy
measures for calibrated building models \citep{Bacher2011RCIdentification}.
Downstream control utility is reported with the maintenance score~\eqref{eq:m-ms}
\citep{Wang2025SafeDRL}, the comfort-violation fraction, and heating energy,
each benchmarked against BOPTEST's built-in baseline controller. Because a policy
trained on a surrogate is deployed on a different (live) system, the central
methodological risk is dataset/distribution shift \citep{Quinonero2009DatasetShift};
the evaluation therefore always reports the \emph{live}-emulator score, never only the
surrogate-internal score. The transfer-gap diagnostics used later ($\Delta m_s$, action-gap norm, and first divergence step) operationalize this same simulator-induced distribution-shift idea for the surrogate-to-BOPTEST setting \citep{Quinonero2009DatasetShift,RiahiSamani2026OOD}; they are diagnostic adaptations, not universal benchmark metrics. For transferability, each hypothesis (H1--H4), pass threshold, and recalibration adapter is pre-specified \emph{before} the corresponding runs; full reproducibility details are in the Supplementary Material. Cross-test-case transfer is realised through a light recalibration adapter
rather than assumed zero-shot, following the evidence that transfer is
source-dependent \citep{Hou2024MultiSource}. Controller scores are reported with
seed statistics wherever repeated-seed runs are available: the central fidelity--utility
backends (pure BB, matched-resolution BB, direct GB, and the hybrid) are evaluated over $N=3$
seeds (seed s.d.\ shown where available; Fig.~\ref{fig:paradox_scatter}, Table~\suppref{tab:seed_band})
and MORL over the five fixed seeds $\{42,43,44,45,46\}$, whereas the HDRL censor-weight
sweep remains a deterministic single-seed run, a limitation stated explicitly in Section~\ref{ssec:b3lim}. The statistical protocol is summarized here; the corresponding results are reported in
Sections~\ref{sec:results1-digital-twin}--\ref{sec:results3-transfer}.

\paragraph{Implementation.}\label{sec:setup}\label{ssec:s-testbed}\label{ssec:s-data}\label{ssec:s-train}
All controllers are evaluated on BOPTEST \citep{Blum2021BOPTEST,Wetter2014Modelica,Arroyo2021GymBOPTEST}, using \texttt{bestest\_air} as the primary testcase and a \texttt{bestest\_hydronic} family for transfer (Section~\ref{sec:results3-transfer}), at a fixed \SI{15}{\minute} control period through one HTTP contract behind which four interchangeable backends sit (live BOPTEST, BB, GB, hybrid), so only the dynamics backend changes across the surrogate-versus-live comparison, and the surrogate backends run \numrange{85.0}{220.2}$\times$ faster than the live loop (a CPU-measured throughput benchmark; hybrid $85.0\times$, calibrated GB $114.2\times$, pure BB $220.2\times$; Supplementary Table~\suppref{tab:speed}), making policy-gradient training tractable on commodity hardware. Surrogates use three corpora generated by scripted excitation: a canonical BB corpus ($51{,}200$ at \SI{1}{\hour}), a $15$-minute exploration corpus ($48{,}384$ at \SI{900}{\second}), and a GB calibration corpus ($10{,}744$ at \SI{900}{\second}); the deliberate one-hour/fifteen-minute pairing is the instrument behind the matched-corpus ablation. BB ($8{,}482$ parameters) is fit by multi-step rollout regression; GB is identified by the Stage~A/B/C inverse calibration of Section~\ref{ssec:m-surrogate} \citep{Bacher2011RCIdentification}, yielding $\Czon=\SI{4.413e5}{\joule\per\kelvin}$ ($+5.1\%$ over the physical prior). All controllers are trained with PPO \citep{Schulman2017PPO} in Stable-Baselines3~2.1.0 \citep{Raffin2021SB3} under Python~3.11.9; the hybrid loads the frozen GB as the censor of Eq.~\eqref{eq:m-hybrid}. All experiments ran on a laptop workstation (AMD Ryzen~7 7445HS, 6~cores/12~threads, 3.2~GHz base; 32~GB RAM; NVIDIA RTX~4050 Laptop GPU) under Windows~11 Pro, with the BOPTEST RTE deployed as a Docker container; the surrogate-versus-live speedup was timed on CPU. Canonical targeted-window KPI tables use seed~42; the central BB, matched-resolution BB, direct-GB, and hybrid thermostatic comparison additionally carries an $N=3$ seed-robustness audit (Table~\suppref{tab:seed_band}); the central paradox claim thus receives seed replication while the boundary tests receive effort proportional to their role (the HDRL mechanistic control remains single-seed and MORL is evaluated over $N=5$ fixed seeds with stated variance); all are benchmarked against BOPTEST's built-in PI \citep{Wang2025SafeDRL}. Each hypothesis (H1--H4) is pre-specified \emph{before} its runs; full reproduction details are in the Supplementary Material.

\section{Experimental Results}\label{sec:experimental}
The experiments answer four questions in sequence. \emph{First}, which surrogate is the more faithful predictor? The calibrated GB RC--NeuralODE, by a wide margin (\S\ref{sec:results1-digital-twin}). \emph{Second}, does that higher fidelity make it the better reinforcement-learning training environment? No: used directly, both GB and a matched-resolution BB collapse on the live runtime (\S\ref{sec:results2-control}), which is the paper's central causal test. \emph{Third}, why do the high-fidelity backends collapse, and how is control recovered? Their action-response surfaces are rougher, which drives policy saturation and live collapse, whereas a role-separated hybrid (smooth BB rollouts plus a frozen GB reward censor) restores it (\S\ref{sec:mechanism-hybrid}). \emph{Fourth}, how far does the result transfer? It is controller-family specific (\S\ref{sec:results2-boundary}), and the inverse-calibration pipeline transfers across buildings while the frozen policy does not (\S\ref{sec:results3-transfer}). These last two subsections are \emph{boundary tests} that map the scope of the paradox rather than re-proving it. The corresponding reproducibility details are retained in the Supplementary Material.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The experimental sequence closes the paper's four claims without treating every controller family as part of the primary paradox. First, calibrated GB is the best predictor, but direct use of GB and matched-resolution BB as PPO rollout environments falsifies the assumption that predictive fidelity alone implies training utility. Second, the hybrid backend supports the role-separation hypothesis: BB remains the smooth rollout substrate and frozen GB supplies only a reward-shaping disagreement censor. Third, HDRL and MORL show that this regularization is controller-family specific and observation-dependent. Fourth, hydronic transfer is component-level: inverse calibration transfers more reliably than the frozen policy.

\textbf{Predictive fidelity and training utility are distinct, and over part of the range opposed.} The calibrated twin GB is the far better predictor (24\,h rollout \RMSE{} $0.644$ vs $1.557\,^{\circ}$C) yet, used directly as the training environment, yields an unusable controller (seed-42 $m_s=1.046$; $N=3$ mean $1.071$, seed-stable collapse; $>77\%$ violation), while the weaker BB yields a usable one ($m_s=0.073$/$0.095$). A matched-resolution closed-loop ablation settles the obvious confound: the \emph{same} black-box BB retrained at $15$ minutes is strictly \emph{more accurate} ($0.876\,^{\circ}$C) yet also collapses ($m_s=1.14$/$1.21$; Table~\ref{tab:paradox}), so the operative variable is the fidelity/smoothing trade-off induced by temporal resolution, not the model class, and a matched-corpus decomposition splits the fidelity gain into a corpus-resolution term and a Stage~A/B/C calibration term whose relative size is decomposition-path-dependent, so it is reported symmetrically rather than as a single number ($25.4\%$ calibration under the additive matched-BB split versus $90.1\%$ under the raw-GB-backbone split; Section~\ref{sec:results1-digital-twin}). This is interpreted as an instance of model exploitation in model-based RL, a distribution-shift effect \citep{Quinonero2009DatasetShift,RiahiSamani2026OOD}: the sharper response surface of the high-fidelity backends ($7.9$--$9.4\times$ rougher; Supplementary Table~\suppref{tab:surface_sharpness}) is exploited by policy-gradient search into a near bang-bang law that does not survive transfer. A $\Delta t$-rescaling control isolates the operative property: a backend with the \emph{same} scale-free roughness but a fine per-step increment also collapses (Table~\suppref{tab:seed_band}), so what matters is the coarse increment magnitude, not smoothness alone.

\textbf{Role separation, not ensembling, is what makes the hybrid work.} Separating the two roles (BB for smooth dynamics, a frozen GB as a reward-shaping censor) recovers the best cross-window robustness ($m_s=0.087$/$0.041$, sub-$5\%$ violation on both windows) at an $85\times$ training speed-up. The hybrid should therefore not be read as a generic ensemble of two predictors: if it were an accuracy-oriented ensemble, GB would naturally dominate the rollout. The experiments show the opposite. The physically richer model is useful only after its role is restricted to a detached model-disagreement censor, while the smoother BB remains the training substrate.

\textbf{The physics-regularisation recipe is controller-family specific, and transfer resolves into a component-level boundary.} The optimal censor weight is $\lambda_{\mathrm{temp}}=0.10$ for thermostatic PPO but $0$ for the hierarchical agent, which already encodes enough forecast/structural context that an external censor over-constrains it \citep{Liao2025HDRL,Byeon2025MaxMinMORL}, so a single ``best'' regularisation strength cannot be reported without naming its controller family. This falsification of $\lambda$-universality rests on the two independently swept families (PPO versus HDRL); the $17$D MORL agent adopts $\lambda_{\mathrm{temp}}=0$ by analogy with the HDRL sweep and is not counted as independent evidence for the claim. For transfer, the calibration \emph{pipeline} ports (rollout \RMSE{} down $60.2$--$87.8\%$ on the hydronic family; $\Czon$ re-identifies at a near-uniform $1.918\pm0.032\times$ ratio across an order-of-magnitude volume change (a family-level regularity across three cases, not a claimed physical invariant)), whereas the frozen \emph{policy} does not transfer uniformly: the commercial case passes the $1.25\times$-PI comfort threshold but at a $35.3\%$ energy penalty, while the residential cases fail it zero-shot. The contribution is thus less a new controller than a corrected, quantified account of \emph{why} surrogate-trained controllers succeed or fail, under a pre-specified protocol that lets H1/H3 be stated as \emph{falsified} rather than quietly dropped.

\paragraph{Limitations.}\label{ssec:block1-limitations}\label{ssec:b3lim}
Evaluation is simulation-only on a single-zone \texttt{bestest\_air} emulator, with multi-zone and richer topologies untested and transfer limited to a hydronic family. Several caveats bound the central claim. First, the fidelity--utility relationship is established at three discrete operating points (hourly BB, matched $15$-min BB, and the calibrated GB) rather than as a continuous fidelity--utility curve: the seed replication ($N=3$, seeds $\{42,43,44\}$) shows that the usable controllers, the matched-resolution collapse, and the direct-GB collapse are all seed-stable (Table~\suppref{tab:seed_band}), but the fidelity axis is still sampled at three discrete operating points rather than as a distribution, and an intermediate-resolution sweep (e.g.\ $1800$\,s) is left to future work. Second, the only comparison baseline is BOPTEST's built-in PI, a relatively weak reference, so controller utility is established comparatively against a modest baseline rather than against a strong tuned rule-based, Guideline-36, or MPC controller on the same RC model. Third, the surrogate power channel is reproduced less accurately than temperature (calibrated power-head MAE $482$\,W, about $9\%$ of $P_{\max}=5500$\,W, rising to ${\sim}2.3$\,kW in the partial-calibration diagnostic regime); the reported energy KPIs (Supplementary Figure~\suppref{fig:live_kpi}) and the transfer $\Delta E$ (Table~\ref{tab:transfer_matrix}, e.g.\ the commercial $+35.3\%$) are live-versus-live BOPTEST measurements and do not inherit this surrogate error, but the weaker power channel limits predictive-fidelity claims about the power head and adds noise to the training-time energy reward, which uses the surrogate power prediction, and a cumulative surrogate-versus-BOPTEST energy balance for at least one window is left to future work. Fourth, the controller-family specificity of the censor weight that falsifies H3 rests on a non-uniform sweep density (a dense four-point $\lambda_{\mathrm{temp}}$ sweep for HDRL but only two measured endpoints for thermostatic PPO and a single adopted configuration for MORL), so it is read as consistent with family-specific regularisation rather than as a fully-swept per-family optimum. Finally, the paradox mechanism is \emph{measured} statically on each surrogate's one-step action map and corroborated by the matched-resolution ablation, but a loss-landscape analysis along the PPO trajectory and generality to off-policy algorithms (SAC~\citep{Haarnoja2018SAC}, TD3) remain open; statistical support otherwise varies by family (MORL $N=5$, neutral $m_s=0.187\pm0.078$ with CI far below the PI $0.910$; HDRL single-seed), and the backend$\times$observation grid is only partially crossed.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This study asked whether a surrogate with higher predictive fidelity is automatically a
better environment in which to train a reinforcement-learning HVAC controller, and
tested the question across three controller families and a hydronic family of
transfer testcases under a pre-specified protocol. The answer is
no. Only the temporally coarse, hourly-trained black-box surrogate (BB) trains a
usable controller: the most accurate physical twin (GB; $0.644\,^{\circ}$C 24-hour
rollout \RMSE) collapses on every seed (seed-42 $m_s = 1.046$; $N=3$ mean $1.071$, seed-stable; comfort violation above $77\%$), and the
\emph{same} black-box architecture retrained at the finer $900$\,s resolution becomes
a \emph{more} accurate predictor ($0.876\,^{\circ}$C) yet also fails as a training
environment ($m_s = 1.14$/$1.21$, violation above $85\%$), a fidelity--utility
paradox (H1 falsified) whose operative variable is the surrogate's coarse per-step
increment magnitude (temporal resolution), not the surrogate model class; a matched-corpus
attribution splits the predictive gain into corpus-resolution and calibration terms whose
relative size is decomposition-path-dependent ($25.4\%$ vs $90.1\%$ calibration across the two splits) and is reported symmetrically, not as a single number. Separating the two roles resolves it: using the
black-box surrogate for smooth rollout dynamics and a frozen physical twin as a
reward-shaping censor (H2 supported) recovers the best cross-window robustness of the
study ($m_s = 0.041$ typical, violation below $5\%$) at an $85\times$ training
speed-up. The censor strength is controller-family specific
($\lambda_{\mathrm{temp}}=0.10$ for thermostatic PPO versus $0$ for the hierarchical
agent; H3 falsified by these two independently swept families, the MORL setting adopted by analogy), and transferability resolves into a component-level
boundary (H4): the inverse-calibration pipeline transfers; rollout \RMSE{} falls
by $60.2$--$87.8\%$ and the zone capacitance re-identifies at a near-uniform
ratio ($1.918\pm0.032\times$ the source), whereas the frozen control policy
does not transfer zero-shot. The contribution is therefore less a new controller
than a corrected, quantified account of why surrogate-trained controllers succeed
or fail, together with a reusable role-separating recipe.

Several future directions follow directly from the limitations of
Section~\ref{ssec:b3lim}: extending the study from a single zone to multi-zone and
mixed-topology buildings; extending the now-measured static response-surface
roughness (Supplementary Table~\suppref{tab:surface_sharpness}) to a formal loss-landscape analysis
along the full PPO training trajectory;
improving the heating-power channel of the surrogate so it is a validated rather
than an auxiliary output; broadening transfer beyond the hydronic family and
pairing the transferable calibration pipeline with light policy adaptation instead
of zero-shot reuse; and, ultimately, closing the remaining sim-to-real gap through
deployment on a physical building.
