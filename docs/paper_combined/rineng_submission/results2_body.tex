\subsection{Higher fidelity destroys RL utility}
\label{sec:results2-control}



\emph{Higher predictive fidelity does not make a better RL training environment.} Used directly as PPO rollout environments, both the more accurate GB and a matched-resolution BB collapse on the live runtime ($m_s>1.0$), whereas the less accurate hourly BB trains a usable controller. This is the central causal test of the paper, built on the asymmetric premise that BB is the weaker predictor while the calibrated GB RC--NeuralODE is the stronger one. It compares four training backends under the same thermostatic PPO controller: coarse BB, matched-resolution BB, direct GB, and the hybrid BB/GB backend. The aim is not to compare all possible HVAC controllers, but to test whether higher surrogate predictive fidelity improves downstream live BOPTEST utility.

All policies in this central test are trained on surrogate backends and then evaluated in closed loop against the live BOPTEST \texttt{bestest\_air} runtime. Two targeted 14-day windows are used: \texttt{peak\_heat\_window} (January, daily-mean ambient $-24.4\,^\circ$C) and \texttt{typical\_heat\_window} (February, $+2.4\,^\circ$C). HDRL, MORL, and the yearly PI reference are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results2-boundary} as boundary tests: they identify whether the hybrid regularization and observation design transfer across controller families, not as additional proof of the primary paradox.

Figure~\suppref{S4} summarizes the pipeline. Direct GB is a negative control, not a failed implementation: it asks whether the highest-fidelity predictive surrogate can be used directly as the policy rollout environment (it cannot). The hybrid backend then asks whether the same physical model becomes useful when its role changes from dynamics provider to reward-shaping censor: yes for thermostatic PPO, no for HDRL at the same $\lambda_T$, and conditionally yes for MORL once the observation interface is widened from 5D to 17D.






This pipeline is only feasible because the surrogate backends are orders of magnitude faster than the live emulator (Fig.~\suppref{S5}): millions of policy-gradient steps that would take days against BOPTEST complete in hours in-process.





The controller-learning experiment is structured as a sequence of falsifiable controller-learning hypotheses (CL1--CL5), each resolved against live BOPTEST evidence and tabulated in Supplementary Table~\suppref{S30}. These are distinct from the four paper-level hypotheses (H1--H4) of the Introduction: CL1--CL3 are the controller-experiment counterparts of H1--H3, while CL4--CL5 are additional MORL-specific checks; the paper-level transferability hypothesis H4 is closed separately in Section~\ref{sec:results3-transfer}. This pre-specification-style framing is what lets the negative results (direct GB, HDRL over-regularization, MORL seed variance) be read as decisions rather than tuning accidents.

The shared 17D observation, supply-temperature action~\eqref{eq:action_map}, comfort reward, and maintenance score~\eqref{eq:m-ms} are defined in Section~\ref{ssec:m-pomdp}; RMSE$_T$ is reported against the band centre $T^{\star}=22.5\,^\circ$C. The baseline is a single-level thermostatic PPO trained purely on the BB rollout surrogate (1-hour step, $\lambda_T=\lambda_P=0$; SB3 \texttt{MlpPolicy}, 10M surrogate steps, Table~\suppref{S11}) and run \emph{zero-shot} on the live BOPTEST runtime, the reference against which the two negative controls (direct GB, warm-start) and the hybrid are measured. In the hybrid, frozen GB acts only as a per-step reward censor~\eqref{eq:m-hybrid}: across the hybrid traces the mean temperature disagreement is $0.969\,^\circ$C (p95 $2.52$) and the mean power disagreement $708$~W, large enough to shape learning, bounded enough to stay informative.

\paragraph{Thermostatic PPO negative control and hybrid recovery.}

The paradox is summarized as a single measured evidence chain in Figure~\ref{fig:evidence_chain}; Table~\ref{tab:paradox} and Figure~\suppref{S7} give the underlying numbers. Pure BB PPO is already usable ($m_s=0.073$ peak, $0.095$ typical). Direct GB PPO fails catastrophically despite GB's superior predictive-validation fidelity: live violation reaches 77.1\% on peak and 82.4\% on typical, with RMSE above $4.3\,^\circ$C. The hybrid backend resolves the conflict: $m_s=0.087$ on peak and $0.041$ on typical, with violation below $5\%$ on both windows and lower energy than pure BB on the peak window.

\subsection{Mechanism: temporal coarseness, roughness, and saturation}\label{sec:mechanism-hybrid}
\emph{The high-fidelity backends collapse because their action-response surfaces are rougher}, which drives near bang-bang policy saturation and live failure, whereas the role-separated hybrid avoids this by separating smooth rollout from GB-based model-disagreement censoring. The mechanism is the measured chain from surrogate response geometry to policy behaviour to live BOPTEST outcome. This subsection is not an additional controller comparison; it explains why the single-model backends collapse and why the hybrid remains usable.

Figures~\ref{fig:roughness_mechanism} and~\suppref{S9} explain the collapse: direct GB learns a bang-bang law that places extreme actions in temperature-error regimes the live building does not support. The full training-trajectory loss-landscape is not measured, but the surrogate-side roughness and policy-side saturation that drive it \emph{are} (Fig.~\ref{fig:roughness_mechanism}); which micro-cause dominates is deferred to future work. Figure~\ref{fig:roughness_mechanism} is itself sufficient to read the measured mechanism: both collapsing single-model backends are rougher ($9.4\times$ and $7.9\times$ the usable BB), saturate the action (100\% vs.\ 24\% of steps), and fail live BOPTEST ($m_s>1$). The time-domain signature is direct GB saturating and leaving the comfort band while the coarse BB and the hybrid stay in band (Figure~\ref{fig:closed_loop_main}); the Supplementary table records the numerical audit behind these plotted values.

\label{ssec:coarse-graining}

The direct-GB failure could be dismissed as a grey-box model-class artefact; a matched-config control closes this confound. Retraining the \emph{same black-box} BB on the matched 15-minute corpus makes it a strictly \emph{more accurate} predictor (24\,h RMSE $0.876$ vs $1.557\,^\circ$C) yet, trained under the identical pure-BB recipe, it \emph{collapses} in closed loop ($m_s=1.142$/$1.211$, 85.6/91.4\% violation; matched-BB row of Table~\ref{tab:paradox}), on a par with direct GB and far worse than the hourly-trained controller.

This ablation isolates the model-class confound: improving BB's temporal resolution and predictive RMSE does not improve downstream utility; it destroys it, so BB's useful property is not its black-box class alone. Two mechanisms are entangled in this coarse-versus-fine contrast: temporal coarse-graining yields an optimization-friendly (smoother) response surface, but the hourly BB is also deployed with its hourly increment applied unchanged at the $900$~s step (four times the dimensionally-consistent per-step change), so part of both its smoothness and its $1.557\,^\circ$C error is a train/deploy timestep mismatch rather than corpus resolution alone. A $\Delta t$-rescaled control settles which part is load-bearing: keeping the same hourly BB but rescaling its increment to the $900$~s runtime step collapses the controller on every seed ($m_s=1.19$/$1.22$, $N=3$; Table~\suppref{S14}), placing it with the matched-resolution and direct-GB controls. The hourly BB's usability therefore rests on its large, temporally-coarse per-step increment, not on the corpus alone; an intermediate-resolution sweep (e.g.\ $1800$\,s) remains future work. The outcome across the three single-model backends is therefore not a monotonic fidelity--utility curve but a two-level contrast: the coarse hourly BB is usable, while both finer backends collapse (matched-resolution BB $m_s=1.14$/$1.21$ and the still more accurate direct GB $m_s=1.046$ are both unusable, and the ordering inside the collapse region is non-monotonic in accuracy). This reframes the hybrid as an explicit separation of smoothing (BB rollout) from fidelity (frozen GB censor): PPO needs not only accurate predictions but an optimization-friendly training landscape.

 Retrained on three fixed seeds (\{42,43,44\}), each backend keeps its regime: pure BB and the hybrid hold every seed below the $5\%$ violation bar on both windows, while the matched-resolution BB collapses on every seed ($m_s=1.14$/$1.21$, std $<0.001$) and the direct GB collapses on every seed ($m_s=1.07{\pm}0.02$/$1.13{\pm}0.03$ peak/typical), so the usable-versus-collapse verdicts are seed-stable (Supplementary Table~\suppref{S14}, Fig.~\suppref{S9}); the single-seed peak edge of pure BB over the hybrid does not persist across seeds (HDRL single-seed; MORL over $N{=}5$).





 The smoothing is directly measurable on the surrogates, independent of any controller: sweeping each backend's one-step map $\hat T(a_0)$ over a grid of states, the scale-free \emph{relative roughness} (mean curvature $\div$ mean slope) orders cleanly with usability (Table~\suppref{S15}): the usable hourly BB is smoothest ($0.17$), while both collapsing backends are several times rougher ($9.4\times$ matched-resolution BB, $7.9\times$ GB). Relative roughness is necessary but not sufficient, however: a constant $\Delta t$-rescaling preserves the curvature/slope ratio, so the $\Delta t$-rescaled hourly BB has the \emph{same} relative roughness ($0.17$) as the usable hourly BB yet collapses (Table~\suppref{S14}). The operative surrogate property is therefore the coarse per-step increment magnitude (temporal coarseness), of which relative roughness measures one facet, rather than scale-free smoothness alone. This is the measured surrogate-side counterpart of the policy-side near-bang-bang action gap (Figure~\ref{fig:roughness_mechanism} and Fig.~\suppref{S8}); the raw slope/range are deliberately not used as the metric, being confounded by the step length.





\subsection{Hybrid robustness and controller-family boundary tests}\label{ssec:hybrid-recovery}

The canonical hybrid operating point was selected by a thermostatic sweep over the temperature-disagreement weight $\lambda_T\in\{0.05,0.10,0.15\}$ at fixed $\lambda_P=5\times10^{-5}$ (Table~\suppref{S16}). The mid setting $\lambda_T=0.10$ is canonical: it retains the energy advantage over pure BB while avoiding the stronger comfort degradation seen at the weaker ($0.05$) and stronger ($0.15$) censor settings. Only the canonical point's live-BOPTEST KPIs are retained as a frozen artifact; the bracketing points ($\lambda_T=0.05$ and $0.15$) served selection only and were not carried to the full live two-window protocol, which is why the controller-family $\lambda_T$ comparison legitimately shows thermostatic PPO at its two live-evaluated points, $\lambda_T=0$ (pure BB) and $\lambda_T=0.10$ (hybrid).





A second negative control tests whether GB helps as a policy \emph{initializer} rather than a reward censor: a thermostatic policy pretrained on GB and warm-start-finetuned on live BOPTEST is compared against an otherwise identical scratch finetune (Table~\suppref{S17}). It does not help; warm-starting raises $m_s$ by two to three times on both windows: GB belongs in the reward as a censor, not in the weights as an initializer.





\paragraph{Transfer-gap diagnostics.}

Transfer-gap diagnostics confirm the same ordering from the surrogate side: direct GB shows by far the largest surrogate-to-live mismatch (a learned bang-bang exploit whose policy sits at the opposite action bound from BOPTEST throughout, $g_a\approx2.0$), while the hybrid shows the smallest and stays BOPTEST-consistent longest. The per-controller diagnostics (temperature gap, surrogate-vs-live $\Delta m_s$, action-gap norm, and first divergence step) and their protocol are reported in Table~\suppref{S18}.





\paragraph{HDRL, MORL, and the PI reference.}\label{sec:results2-boundary}

\emph{The model-disagreement censor recipe is controller-family specific.} The preceding sections establish the paradox and its hybrid remedy within the thermostatic PPO family; two further families test whether the recipe transfers. These are boundary tests, not additional proof of the paradox, so their detailed sweeps, architectures, pipelines, and seed analyses are kept in the Supplementary Material.

\paragraph{HDRL: censor-weight specificity.} A seasonal hierarchy routes control to winter/summer PPO setpoint specialists that share the 17D observation and supply-temperature action (Figure~\suppref{S12}). The thermostatic-optimal censor weight $\lambda_T=0.10$ does \emph{not} transfer: because each specialist is already comfort-aware, the GB temperature censor over-regularizes the low-level loop and $m_s$ degrades monotonically with $\lambda_T$ (peak violation roughly quadruples, $6.1\%\to23.0\%$). HDRL is best at $\lambda_T=0$, keeping only the power channel ($\lambda_P=5\times10^{-5}$); the correct censor strength is therefore family-specific, not a universal weight (Table~\suppref{S19}, Fig.~\suppref{S13}).

\paragraph{MORL: observation geometry and seed stability.} A four-stage MORL pipeline (surrogate pretrain $\to$ preference adaptation $\to$ live-BOPTEST finetune $\to$ yearly evaluation) on the power-only hybrid backend is viable only once the observation is widened from 5D to 17D: the current-code 5D interface fails ($\mathrm{RMSE}_T=2.721\,^\circ$C, $37.8\%$ violation, $m_s=0.680$) while the 17D forecast-augmented vector recovers a usable policy ($\mathrm{RMSE}_T=0.720\,^\circ$C, $4.9\%$, $m_s=0.099$). The bottleneck is observation geometry, not the reward scalarization, consistent with forecast-augmented RL-HVAC control~\citep{Gao2024Predictive}. MORL yields a usable comfort--energy Pareto front, but an $N=5$ seed extension turns the canonical operating points into wide distributions (CV $0.42$--$0.61$) that fail a pre-specified stability test, promising in mean but not deployment-stable without explicit stabilization (the 5D$\to$17D observation change is shown schematically in Figure~\suppref{S15} and quantified in Figure~\suppref{S16}; the $N=5$ Pareto seed analysis is retained in the Supplementary Material, Tables~\suppref{S20}--\suppref{S21}).

\paragraph{Synthesis against the PI reference.} The BOPTEST built-in PI controller is a reproducible reference, comfort-poor but energy-frugal (12-month means: $\mathrm{RMSE}_T=3.40\,^\circ$C, $63.6\%$ violation, $m_s=0.910$). All evaluated RL/MORL agents reach $m_s$ well below PI, dominating it on comfort at comparable or lower energy. The cross-family maintenance-score decomposition (Supplementary Table~\suppref{S29}) makes the pattern explicit: the failures (direct GB, PI) carry a large time-in-violation term $r_{\mathrm{time}}\gtrsim0.6$ (PI by chronic under-heating, direct GB by saturated overshoot), whereas the usable controllers (pure BB, hybrid, MORL 17D) keep both $r_{\mathrm{time}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{sev}}$ small.

The controller-learning experiments establish four claims. (i)~Predictive fidelity $\neq$ training utility: direct GB is the more accurate twin yet fails as a rollout environment ($>77\%$ violation), and the matched-resolution ablation (matched-BB row of Table~\ref{tab:paradox}) shows the \emph{same} black-box BB also collapses once its resolution (and accuracy) rises, isolating the fidelity/smoothing trade-off, not the model class. (ii)~Role separation works: BB supplies smooth rollout dynamics and frozen GB a disagreement censor, giving the hybrid ($m_s=0.087$/$0.041$). (iii)~The censor strength is controller-family specific (HDRL best at $\lambda_T=0$). (iv)~MORL is viable only with the 17D interface, with high $N=5$ seed variance. The fixed \texttt{bestest\_air} recipe is then transferred to the hydronic BOPTEST family.
