\subsection{Transfer boundary: calibration transfers, frozen policy does not}
\label{sec:results3-transfer}

\subsubsection{Protocol, testcases, and metrics}

\emph{Transfer is component-level:} the inverse-calibration pipeline ports across the hydronic family (rollout RMSE reduced $60.2$--$87.8\%$; $\Czon$ re-identified at a near-uniform $1.918\pm0.032\times$), whereas the frozen policy transfers only in some regimes. The predictive-validation and central-control experiments established the source-case recipe on BOPTEST \texttt{bestest\_air}: a BB rollout surrogate supplies smooth control-oriented dynamics, a calibrated GB RC--NeuralODE supplies a frozen physics-informed model-disagreement censor, and the thermostatic PPO policy trained on the resulting hybrid backend is the strongest verified controller on the targeted windows. This transferability experiment does not test universal controller generalization. It decomposes transfer into two components: frozen-policy transfer and surrogate-recalibration transfer. This distinction prevents a misleading binary conclusion.

The claim is deliberately bounded: the transferability experiment evaluates transfer to three pre-selected single-zone hydronic cases under documented actuator adapters and pre-specified recalibration, with target-testcase controller fine-tuning explicitly excluded, separating \emph{controller} transfer from \emph{surrogate-recalibration} transfer so the evidence is reported component-wise.








The transferability hypotheses, thresholds, and adapters were pre-specified before any non-\texttt{bestest\_air} run, so every number below was predictable but not predicted; full reproduction details are in the Supplementary Material.

\paragraph{Testcases, actuator adapters, and recalibration regimes.}

The three target testcases span an increasing difficulty ladder relative to \texttt{bestest\_air}; all are single-zone or single-zone-like, heating-capable, and structurally non-identical to the source case.



A pre-specified per-testcase adapter $\mathcal{A}_k$ maps the frozen policy's supply-temperature command (Eq.~\eqref{eq:action_map}) to each target's BOPTEST override inputs, committed and smoke-tested for directional validity before any run; the recalibration regimes keep the controller frozen and vary only how much of the surrogate adapts (adapter definitions and regimes in Supplementary Tables~\suppref{S23}--\suppref{S24}, Figure~\suppref{S20}).










The three targets differ in heat source, distribution path, and actuator set, but share the same single-zone energy balance (Figure~\suppref{S21}). For a hydronic zone the governing first-order balance is
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{zon}}\frac{dT_{\mathrm{zon}}}{dt} = \dot{Q}_{\mathrm{hyd}}(u_t) + \frac{T_{\mathrm{amb}}-T_{\mathrm{zon}}}{R_{\mathrm{env}}} + \dot{Q}_{\mathrm{gain}},
 \label{eq:hydronic_balance}
\end{equation}
where $\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{hyd}}$ is the delivered hydronic heat (whose actuation path, heat pump, boiler/radiator, or district valve, changes per testcase), $R_{\mathrm{env}}$ the envelope resistance, and $\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{gain}}$ the internal/solar gains. Transfer changes the \emph{delivery path} $\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{hyd}}$ and the actuator interface, but $C_{\mathrm{zon}}$ is the shared lumped physical coordinate that Stage~B re-identifies, which is why its re-identified value transfers (Section~\ref{ssec:czon}).



Table~\suppref{S25} summarizes the identified physics. The equivalent thermal mass $C_{\mathrm{zon}}/c_{p,\mathrm{air}}$ is roughly $2\times$ the \texttt{bestest\_air} value for every hydronic case (added water-loop and surface mass). Mean delivered power exposes the regime: approximately $0.7$--$2.8$~kW for the residential cases (conduction/slow-hydronic dominated) but $76.9$~kW for the commercial case (ventilation/AHU-dominated), about $28\times$ the heat-pump target, the physical reason the commercial controller passes comfort yet overshoots energy by $+35.3\%$.





The controller-side pass threshold is normalized by each testcase's built-in PI baseline ($\tau=1.25\,m_{s,\mathrm{PI}}$, with the $1.25$ value pre-specified rather than chosen post hoc); energy is reported on a separate axis as the percentage change against PI; surrogate-side transfer is the relative rollout-RMSE improvement after full Stage~A/B/C recalibration; and the physical transfer diagnostic is the re-identified capacitance ratio $\rho_C$ against the source value $C_{\mathrm{air}}=4.413\times10^5$~J/K (all column formulas in the caption of Table~\ref{tab:transfer_matrix}). Two-axis reporting ($m_s$ threshold and $\Delta E$) is necessary because the commercial testcase passes the $m_s$ bound while consuming substantially more energy than PI.

\subsubsection{Controller-side transfer verdicts}

Table~\ref{tab:transfer_matrix} contains two stories. The surrogate component transfers strongly: full Stage~A/B/C improves RMSE by $60.2$--$87.8\%$ on all three targets. The frozen controller does not transfer uniformly: both residential cases fail the $1.25\times$-PI threshold, while the commercial stretch passes it at a $+35.3\%$ energy penalty. This component-level boundary (identified physics transfers, the frozen control law does not) is consistent with building heterogeneity requiring local adaptation rather than zero-shot reuse~\citep{Coraci2024HeteroTL,Coraci2025HiLo}.

The three testcases confirm the component-level split (Table~\ref{tab:transfer_matrix}, Figure~\ref{fig:deployment_plane}). Both residential cases fail the $1.25\times$-PI controller threshold while \emph{saving} energy (heat-pump $m_s=0.665$ at $-7.3\%$; boiler $m_s=0.976$ at $-5.8\%$), yet full Stage~A/B/C recalibration succeeds on both surrogates (RMSE$_T$ to $0.565$ and $0.335\,^\circ$C; $60.2$/$87.4\%$ gains; $C_{\mathrm{zon}}=1.892$/$1.954\times$). The pre-specified commercial ``stretch'' case is mixed: the frozen controller \emph{passes} the $m_s$ threshold ($0.431$ vs PI $0.628$) but only at $+35.3\%$ energy (threshold-pass, not deployment-ready), while its surrogate side is again strong ($87.8\%$ RMSE gain, $C_{\mathrm{zon}}=1.909\times$, contradicting the version-locked pre-specified scale-dependent $[3,10]\times$ prediction). Across all three, surrogate recalibration transfers; the frozen controller does not transfer uniformly.











\subsubsection{Surrogate-side transfer}
\label{ssec:czon}

The surrogate component transfers on all three targets: full Stage~A/B/C cuts rollout RMSE by $60.2$--$87.8\%$, and the re-identified zone capacitance clusters at $\rho_C=1.918\pm0.032\times$ the source, read as an \emph{effective} single-zone RC signature rather than a physical invariant, a curious $N=3$ regularity, not a law. The full $C_{\mathrm{zon}}$-consistency analysis, the surrogate-side recalibration curves, and the pre-specified hypothesis closure and predictions are reported in the Supplementary Material (Figure~\suppref{S28}; Tables~\suppref{S25}, \suppref{S27}, \suppref{S34}, and~\suppref{S28}).
